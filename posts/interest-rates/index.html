Monthly Interest Rates

Converting annual interest rates to monthly interest rates is done with the 12th root, not dividing by 12.

Posted 2026-11-12
By Mikel Mcdaniel
3 min read

The way interest rates are calculated for credit cards and mortgages is broken in a way that is subtle. Interest rates are often given annually. For example, the currently common interest rate of about 24% for many credit cards should mean that if you have \$100 of debt and don’t pay anything and just let it gain interest, then in exactly one year you will have \$100 * 124% = \$100 * 1.24 = \$124 of debt. That makes perfect sense.

The Wrong Way

Interest rates are usually applied per monthly. There are 12 months in a year, so the monthly interest rate is calculated as 24% / 12 months = 2% / month. If you start with \$100 of debt, then after 1 month, you have \$102 of debt… and after 12 you should of course have \$124 of debt exactly as if the interest had been applied annually.

Yikes. 2% per month actually means \$\approx$ 26.82% per year. The issue is that dividing X by 12 tells you what number you need to add 12 times to get X but when we calculate the next month’s debt, we multiply instead of adding.

The Correct Way

Luckily the solution is relatively simple. Instead of dividing by 12, we need to take the 12th root. For a 24% annual interest, the monthly interest should actually be \$\sqrt[12]{124\%} = \sqrt[12]{1.24} \approx 1.01809 \approx 101.809\%$.

Mortgage Example

If you were to buy a house in 2025 in the US, you might hope to find a \$125,000 house, and take a \$100,000 loan on the house (assuming you put a 20\% downpayment on the house, which is common). Maybe you got an annual interest rate of 5\% for a 30-year mortgage. That means you start with \$100k in debt and each month 5\%/12 interest is added even though it should be $\sqrt[12]{105\%} - 100\%$.

This is the difference between a \$536.82 and \$530.06 per month or $\$536.82 - \$530.06 * 360 = \$2,435.91$ in total over 30 years.
